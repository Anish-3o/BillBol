The problem

The busiest moment at the counter still runs on pen, paper and memory.

Bills by hand

In the evening rush, totals are added on a calculator or in a notebook while the queue waits.

Udhaar in a diary

Credit to regular customers lives in a notebook. Reminders are awkward, and money gets forgotten.

Apps built for typing

Most billing software expects English typing at a desk. The counter has neither.

The insight

Every order is already spoken out loud.

“Bhaiya, do kilo cheeni, ek namak aur teen Maggi.”

BillBol turns that sentence into a bill.

How it works

A voice bill in four steps

1

“Hey Ramu”

Ramu listens hands-free at the counter, or the shopkeeper taps the mic.

2

Speak the order

In Hindi, Hinglish or Marathi. Items and quantities are matched to the shop’s own products and prices.

3

Ramu reads it back

Each item and the total, calculated by the app, are read aloud. It can take the customer’s name for udhaar.

4

“Haan” saves it

Only a spoken or tapped yes saves the bill, updates stock and prints the receipt.

Why shopkeepers can trust it

AI drafts. The shopkeeper decides.

Nothing saves without a yes

Voice and scanned items stay a draft until a person confirms. Stock, money and invoices never change on the AI’s word.

AI never does the maths

Totals, GST and summaries are calculated in code. The AI only turns real numbers into friendly sentences.

Exact to the paisa

Money is stored as whole paise, never decimals, so a ₹131 bill can never become ₹130.99.
